I am **Ka Lok (Antony) Kwok**, born and raised in Hong Kong and currently pursuing a Master of Science (M.S.) in Environmental Policy and Planning at Tufts University. I previously earned an M.S. in Economics from Tufts University, as well as a B.S. in Economics, a B.A. in Mathematics, and a minor in Statistics from the University of Minnesota Twin Cities.

My research interests lie at the intersection of **urban economics, housing policy, transportation accessibility, spatial inequality, urban informatics, and applied and spatial econometrics**. My broader academic interests extend to the economics of education, labor economics, public policy, and socioeconomic inequality.

I am particularly interested in understanding how **affordable housing policies, urban development, and transportation systems shape neighborhood outcomes, spatial disparities, and access to socioeconomic opportunities**. My work explores the relationships between affordable housing, neighborhood health, transportation equity, and the distribution of resources and opportunities across communities. I am especially interested in how place-based policies affect disadvantaged populations and whether their benefits extend beyond the communities or developments they directly serve.

Through my ongoing research and interdisciplinary training, I seek to integrate **econometric methods, geographic information systems (GIS), spatial analysis, and large-scale urban data** to investigate these questions. My broader goal is to contribute rigorous empirical evidence that can inform more equitable housing, transportation, health, and urban development policies.

## Beyond Academia: From Athletics to Research

Before pursuing my academic interests, I dedicated much of my childhood and adolescence to competitive sports in Hong Kong. I began playing tennis at a young age and was selected for training by coaches during primary school. Despite my commitment to the sport, limited financial support eventually made it difficult to continue along that path.

I subsequently transitioned to karate, where I achieved success in domestic competitions and eventually had the opportunity to **represent Hong Kong internationally as a member of its karate national team**.

My transition from competitive athletics to higher education and academic research was neither straightforward nor easy. It involved significant challenges, difficult decisions, and a gradual redefinition of my goals and aspirations. These experiences shaped my resilience, perspective, and understanding of how access to resources and opportunities can influence individual life trajectories.
