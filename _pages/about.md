I am **Ka Lok (Antony) Kwok**, born and raised in Hong Kong and currently pursuing a Master of Science (M.S.) in Environmental Policy and Planning at Tufts University. I previously earned an M.S. in Economics from Tufts University, as well as a B.S. in Economics, a B.A. in Mathematics, and a minor in Statistics from the University of Minnesota Twin Cities.

My research interests lie at the intersection of **urban economics, housing policy, transportation accessibility, spatial inequality, urban informatics, and applied and spatial econometrics**. My broader academic interests encompass health policy, economics of education, labor economics, gender inequality, income inequality, and food and social policy.

I am particularly interested in understanding how **affordable housing policies, urban development, and transportation systems shape neighborhood outcomes, spatial disparities, and access to socioeconomic opportunities**. My research examines the relationships between place-based policies, affordable housing, neighborhood health, transportation equity, and socioeconomic well-being, particularly among disadvantaged communities. I am also interested in how policy effects vary across geographic and socioeconomic contexts, including potential spatial spillovers into surrounding neighborhoods.

Drawing on my interdisciplinary training in economics, mathematics, statistics, and urban planning, I integrate **econometric methods, geographic information systems (GIS), spatial analysis, causal inference, and large-scale urban data** to investigate these questions. My goal is to contribute rigorous empirical evidence that informs more equitable and effective urban and social policies.

Beyond academia, my journey has been shaped by an unconventional transition from competitive athletics to academic research. Growing up in Hong Kong, I began playing tennis during childhood and was selected for training by coaches in primary school. After financial constraints made it difficult to continue pursuing tennis competitively, I transitioned to karate, where I achieved success in domestic competitions and eventually represented **Hong Kong internationally as a member of its karate national team**.

My transition from competitive athletics to higher education was far from straightforward. As a first-generation university student, I encountered challenges, setbacks, and difficult decisions that shaped my perspective on education, opportunity, and personal growth. These experiences have influenced both who I am today and the questions that motivate my research.
