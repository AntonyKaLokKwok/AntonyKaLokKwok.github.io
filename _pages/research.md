My research examines how **housing policies, transportation systems, and urban development shape neighborhood conditions, socioeconomic inequality, and access to opportunity**. I combine training in **economics, mathematics, and statistics** with **applied and spatial econometrics, geographic information systems (GIS), causal inference, and computational urban analytics**.

I am pursuing an **M.S. in Environmental Policy and Planning at Tufts University (expected May 2027)** and earned an **M.S. in Economics from Tufts University (May 2025)**. My current research spans affordable housing and housing-market dynamics, transportation equity, neighborhood health, and geographic information inequality.

## Research Interests

- **Urban and housing economics:** affordable housing policy, Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) development, housing-market dynamics, and neighborhood change
- **Transportation accessibility and equity:** public transportation, bikeshare accessibility, and spatial disparities in mobility
- **Spatial and socioeconomic inequality:** neighborhood opportunity, environmental justice, urban heat, and health equity
- **Urban informatics:** Wikipedia and Wikidata, computational urban analytics, and geographic information inequality
- **Applied economics and public policy:** education, labor and income inequality, food policy, and accessibility

## Research in Progress

### The Macroeconomic Effects of LIHTC Development: A Dynamic General-Equilibrium Analysis

*2026–present · Master's thesis · Advisor: Professor Shomon Shamsuddin*

I am developing a calibrated deterministic dynamic general-equilibrium model linking household consumption and saving, nonhousing production, private housing investment, and LIHTC-supported construction. Planned counterfactual simulations will investigate how affordable housing subsidies affect rental prices, private construction, housing supply, aggregate consumption, and investment. The empirical component will draw on HUD, American Community Survey (ACS), U.S. Census, Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), and Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) data, complemented by GIS analysis of LIHTC development and neighborhood socioeconomic conditions.

*Status: Thesis research and model development in progress; counterfactual results are not yet available.*

### What does Wikipedia say about Cities? Epistemology and Spatial Distribution in the World’s Largest Encyclopedia

*2025–present · Collaborative research with Professor Justin Hollander · Accepted for presentation at UAA 2027*

This project investigates differences in the geographic coverage and depth of place-related information on Wikipedia. From a dataset of **1,660,911 Wikipedia pages**, we identified **62,789 geographic entities** using Wikidata classifications, including cities, towns, villages, human settlements, and administrative regions. Our R workflows integrate place classifications, population estimates, and geographic data to examine variation in representation across settlements and regions. Initial descriptive analysis finds a positive correlation between log population and log article length (**r ≈ 0.46**).

### Spatial Inequalities in Bluebikes Accessibility: Affordable Housing, Transportation Disadvantage, and Urban Heat in Boston

*2026–present · Conference research · Accepted for presentation at AAG 2027*

This research examines whether residents near LIHTC developments experience unequal access to Boston's Bluebikes system and how accessibility varies with neighborhood socioeconomic characteristics and urban heat. The analysis combines **HUD LIHTC locations, Bluebikes stations, ACS indicators, and satellite-derived land surface temperature**. Preliminary analysis identifies **47 LIHTC census tracts with Bluebikes stations and 21 without**. Ongoing work extends the analysis through pedestrian-network service areas, slope-adjusted least-cost paths, and spatial econometric methods.

## Selected Research and Applied Projects

### Spatial Analysis of LIHTC Development, Housing Affordability, and Tract-Level Health in Massachusetts

*Spring 2026 · GIS 203: Spatial Statistics · Professor Sumeeta Srinivasan*

Integrated HUD LIHTC records, ACS housing and socioeconomic data, CDC PLACES health estimates, and census tract boundaries to study affordable housing and neighborhood health. Methods included **exploratory spatial data analysis, Local Moran's I (LISA), OLS, residual spatial autocorrelation tests, geographically weighted regression (GWR), and exploratory difference-in-differences**. Cross-sectional analysis found a positive association between LIHTC concentration and high blood pressure prevalence (**β = 0.465, p = 0.0094**), alongside substantial spatial heterogeneity. These observational associations should not be interpreted as causal effects.

**Interactive GIS project:** [Spatial Analysis of LIHTC, Housing Affordability, and Health](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/9cdf1d44cebd4872a843927d8724f69a) — *2026 Tufts GIS Expo, GIS Award Finalist (Advanced StoryMaps).*

### Housing and Transit Vulnerability in Grand Annecy

*Summer 2026 · GIS 201: Introduction to GIS · Professor Sumeeta Srinivasan*

Combined French property transaction data (DVF+), SIBRA GTFS transit schedules, and IRIS statistical boundaries to assess disparities in housing affordability and transit service in Grand Annecy, France. Using **400-meter transit buffers, nearest-stop distances, transit frequency, raster weighted overlay, and spatial autocorrelation**, I constructed a housing–transit vulnerability index. The index showed significant positive spatial clustering (**Global Moran's I = 0.627, p < 0.001**).

### Bluebikes Access, Affordable Housing, and Heat Inequality in Boston

*Spring 2026 · UEP 173: Transportation Planning · Professor Shan Jiang*

Studied the intersections of bikeshare station access, LIHTC housing, neighborhood socioeconomic characteristics, and urban heat across Boston census tracts. Using GIS proximity analysis, logistic regression, OLS, and tract fixed-effects models, I assessed station presence, station distance, land surface temperature, and temperature-related ridership. Results identified differences in station accessibility across LIHTC neighborhoods and a stronger positive temperature–ridership association in LIHTC tracts with station access (**β ≈ 9.92, p < 0.001**).

### Advancing Environmental Justice in Boston's Chinatown: Evaluation and Strategies for Just Energy Transition and Resilient Open Space

*Spring 2026 · UEP 255: Field Projects · Professor Penn Loh · Community partner: Chinatown Community Land Trust (CCLT)*

Worked with a graduate research team and CCLT to assess environmental justice challenges and strategies for equitable energy transitions and resilient open space. Developed **Cantonese-language surveys and interview protocols**, conducted resident interviews in Cantonese, and contributed to community-engaged qualitative research and policy recommendations.

### Mapping Urban Heat Inequality in Boston: Integrating Affordable Housing, Income, and Bluebikes Access

*Fall 2025 · UEP 237: Urban Analytics · Professor Shan Jiang · Presented at AAG 2026*

Constructed a Boston census-tract-by-month panel using **ERA5-Land temperature, Bluebikes trips, HUD LIHTC data, ACS socioeconomic measures, and tree-canopy indicators**. Applied spatial analysis, origin–destination mapping, tract fixed-effects regression, pooled OLS, interaction models, and exploratory GWR. A **1°C increase in county-average monthly temperature** was associated with approximately **7.2 additional Bluebikes trips per 1,000 residents** within a census tract (**p < 0.01**). The temperature–ridership association was weaker in tracts with larger minority population shares.

### Does Gender and Marital Status Affect Workplace Inequality?

*Spring 2025 · EC 202: Econometrics · Professor Matthew Gudgeon*

Analyzed China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) data from 2010–2022 to investigate gender and marital-status differences in earnings. Estimated **high-dimensional fixed-effects regressions**, individual-clustered standard errors, interaction terms, and urban–rural subsample models. The analysis found a significant female earnings disadvantage and additional negative associations between female gender, marital history, and earnings, with larger estimated disparities in urban areas.

### School Financial Reform and Income Inequality

*Spring 2025 · EC 144: Income Inequality · Professor Elizabeth Setren*

Constructed a U.S. state-year panel covering 1990–2018 to study school-finance reform and household income. Applied **difference-in-differences, placebo tests, pre-treatment trend checks, and propensity score matching combined with DiD**. Estimated a negative post-2000 income difference in reform states relative to comparison states, while recognizing limitations associated with varying reform-adoption dates and causal identification.
